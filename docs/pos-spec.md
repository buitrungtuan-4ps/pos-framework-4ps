# Product specification

**Status** Accepted · **Owner** @maintainers-domain · **Last reviewed** 2026-08-18

What the product does. Implementation is in [architecture.md](architecture.md); naming rules are in [naming-and-api.md](naming-and-api.md).

Everything here runs **offline** at the store unless explicitly marked as a cloud feature.

---

## 1. Roles and channels

Sales channels: dine-in, takeaway, delivery marketplaces, external channels via API, and guest QR ordering.
Staff roles ship as editable templates: server, cashier, shift lead, store manager, brand manager, tenant admin, accountant (read-only), auditor (audit log only).

## 2. Tables, tabs, and seats

- Floor plan per area. Table states: `FREE → OCCUPIED → AWAITING_PAYMENT → NEEDS_CLEANING → FREE`, `OCCUPIED → NEEDS_CLEANING` when the guests move to another table before asking for the bill, and `OCCUPIED → FREE` when the table is released with nothing sold on it ([state-machines.md](state-machines.md)).
- **A table seated by mistake is released** ([ADR-0163](adr/0163-a-table-seated-by-mistake-is-released.md)). `POST /api/tables/{id}/release` gives a seated table straight back to the floor while its order has no unvoided line and no bill, and is refused otherwise (`409 ORDER_NOT_EMPTY`): releasing says nobody ate. It writes `sales.order.closed` (the order ends owing nothing) and `sales.table.closed` in one transaction, and needs `sales.table.manage`, as seating does ([ADR-0158](adr/0158-the-till-enforces-each-persons-own-permissions.md)). The order stays in the log as opened and closed with nothing sold, and the table lets go of it: a dish a stale till sends to the table afterwards is refused (`409 NO_OPEN_ORDER`) rather than joining an order that has ended. Guests who asked for the bill and then left are released once a manager voids the bill and the lines are voided.
- **A table has exactly one open order at a time.** This is a state-machine invariant, not a UI convention: a second open order on an occupied table is unrepresentable.
- **Transfer** moves an order to another table; **merge** combines two orders while each line keeps its origin so the kitchen is not confused. *Transfer is built: `POST /api/tables/{id}/transfer` with `{"to_table_id"}` moves the guests' order to a **free** table, under `sales.order.transfer` (servers and up by default; no PIN). It writes one event, `sales.table.transferred`. The table they left goes to `NEEDS_CLEANING`, as it does when guests pay and go, and the new one is seated with the same order: its lines, its fired tickets and its seated time. Nothing is rung or fired again, because a ticket names its order and every screen reads the order's table from where the order is now. **Only before the bill**: a table `AWAITING_PAYMENT` pays where it sits. Refused while the order is a guest's QR order still waiting for staff (§13), because refusing that order hands the table back to the order it displaced, which only works while both are where they opened; staff decide on it first. A kitchen ticket already printed still names the table it was sent from. Merging two tables' orders is not built; the one merge the edge writes is a guest's QR order joining its table's order (§13).*
- **Tabs** (capability flag `tabs_enabled`): an open order identified by a guest name rather than a table — for bars and counters. *Not built: no tab exists yet, so the console does not offer the flag (§10).*
- **Seats** (capability flag `seats_enabled`): each line may carry a seat number, which enables splitting a bill by seat.
- **How long a table has been seated** is part of the floor read. `GET /api/floor` carries `seated_times`: when each seated table's guests sat down, for the tables somebody is sitting at. It is read from the table's order id, a ULID minted at the moment of seating. The floor plan shows it as "Seated 25 min" (`docs/ui-ux.md` §3).

## 3. Ordering

- Add items from the menu; each line carries quantity, modifiers, and a free-text note. **The note's text stays at the store and never enters the event log** — it is exactly where "for Mr Nguyễn, severe peanut allergy" gets typed, and the log is immutable, so anything personal in it could never be erased. Events carry only whether a note exists. The edge holds the text in memory for as long as the line is on a screen, which for a paid order without a table means until the kitchen bumps it or its business day ends ([ADR-0161](adr/0161-a-paid-order-without-a-table-stays-on-the-kitchen-board-until-it-is-done.md)), at most 2,048 notes and 200 characters each, and writes it nowhere: the kitchen reads it on its board and its printed ticket, and a note the edge lost to a restart is shown and printed as lost rather than as nothing ([ADR-0157](adr/0157-a-guest-note-lives-in-the-stores-memory-for-the-service.md)). A delivery or QR order's note is kept the same way. No report, reconciliation or ERP posting has any use for it.
- **Modifier groups** may be required or optional and may nest. A dedicated `SPLIT_ITEM` modifier supports half-and-half products: one line, two halves, configurable pricing (**default: the higher-priced half**), and a bill of materials computed per fraction. *Built so far ([ADR-0127](adr/0127-modifier-groups-reach-the-edge.md)): a **flat** group reaches the store on the `menu` node and the edge refuses a line that breaks its rule — required is `min_select >= 1`, with no second flag. **Nesting** is not authorable (a group's members are items), and `SPLIT_ITEM` changes the shape and pricing of a line rather than being a selection rule, so it gets its own record; both are still open.*
- **Courses** group lines (starter, main, dessert). Fire by course or fire everything. *Built so far ([ADR-0130](adr/0130-a-course-is-something-the-catalog-names.md)): a **course is a catalog entity** — tenant-scoped, named, carrying the `sort` that is the whole meaning of the grouping — authored through `/admin/catalog/courses`, and an item declares which course it goes out on the way it declares its tax class. Everything around it was already built: the event field, the line route, the station routing rule that matches on one and the edge that honours it, the `courses_enabled` capability and the domain's refusal when it is off. The compiled `menu` node now **carries the courses**: an entry declares its `course_id`, the courses ride once on the catalog in service order (ties by id), an archived course is not published and an entry naming one compiles without a course, and a channel carries only the courses something on it goes out on. **Fire by course** is wired end to end: `POST /api/orders/{id}/fire/{course_id}` sends every unsent line on one course in one transaction, `GET /api/menu` carries the courses in service order, and the order screen offers a button per course with food still waiting. `courses_enabled` gates it — a store with courses off draws no course row and the route refuses. A routing rule's `course_id` is checked at the write: `POST /admin/kitchen/routing` refuses one no **active** course carries, so a rule that could never match is caught where the operator is standing rather than published and honoured silently. ADR-0130 is complete.*
- **Fire** sends lines to the kitchen. Before firing, lines are freely editable. After firing, cancelling requires a permission, a reason, and prints a **VOID ticket** at the correct station.
- **Hold** keeps a line unfired. **86** marks an item unavailable: it greys out on every device instantly and tells delivery marketplaces the item is temporarily out. *Built so far: staff mark an item sold out at the till or on the kitchen board, and bring it back (`POST /api/menu/{id}/sold-out`, `POST /api/menu/{id}/restore`), under `sales.item.mark_unavailable` with no PIN — running out moves no money. Each writes `inventory.item.sold_out` (`automatic: false`: a person decided, not a stock threshold) or `inventory.item.restored`; a second tap writes nothing; and the mark lasts until somebody restores it — a restart replays it, and the end of the business day does not clear it. `GET /api/menu` carries `sold_out` beside `available`, so a till can offer to restore what staff marked and leave alone what the console withdrew. The edge refuses a line for a sold-out item, or one choosing a sold-out modifier, whichever way it arrives: from a table or the counter (`409 ITEM_NOT_SELLABLE`), and from a guest's QR order or a marketplace (`failed_precondition`, exactly as for an item the console withdrew — the menu they ordered from was published before the kitchen ran out). Still open: telling the marketplaces, and greying the item on the guest's QR page, which is drawn from the published menu; and **auto-86** from stock (§8).*
- **Open item**: an ad-hoc item with a typed name and price. Separate permission, always audited.
- Every order action is an **append command**, so two devices working on the same table merge cleanly.

## 4. Kitchen

- Lines route to stations by configured rules (one station per item in v1).
- **Kitchen display per station**: cards grouped by order and course, an age timer per card, colour change past a configured threshold. **Bump** completes; **recall** brings back the last 60 seconds. *Built so far: the threshold is each station's `late_after_seconds` on the `stations` node ([ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 2), from 60 to 3600 seconds, which the console sets per station in whole minutes. A station that sets none, or one outside those bounds, is late after 600 seconds, the ten minutes every board used before. The edge sends each station's threshold with the station list the till reads (`GET /api/floor`), and a ticket is late after its own station's threshold, on that station's board and on the board for every station; one whose station the store did not publish is late after ten minutes. Recall is not built.*
- **Expo screen** (optional) aggregates bumped items by table for runners.
- Printing runs a **queue with retry**. A failed station printer falls back to a configured backup printer or raises a red badge on that station's display. Screens are the fallback for paper, and paper is the fallback for screens. *Built so far: a ticket for a station with no printer of its own prints on the printer of the backup station the console names on it, one hop. A ticket prints in the language of the station whose printer prints it, so the backup's in that case, because its cooks read it (§12).*

## 5. Billing and payment

- Bill: `total_due = subtotal − discounts − comps + service_charge + tax + rounding_adjustment`. Service charge is applied **after** discounts and **before** tax, and is taxable per `store.tax.service_charge_taxable` (default true). Tax rounds **per tax-class subtotal**, not per line and not per bill. Cash rounding is an explicit `rounding_adjustment` line, never a silent adjustment, so the printed lines reconcile to the printed total ([ADR-0028](adr/0028-settlement-and-payment-invariant.md)). *Built so far: each tax amount, and each fee, rounds to the currency's minor unit half-up, or toward zero where the store's `locale.tax_rounding` is `TAX_ROUNDING_DOWN` ([configuration register](configuration.md), [ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 2). That is the tax of each class, whether prices include their tax or not, the tax of a charge taxed at a class no line is in, and each fee's amount; a tax line's named parts share its tax as it rounded. The cash rounding of the total is always half-up, because it is about the country's coins, not its tax, and a line's price, a campaign and a recipe's consumption keep their own rounding. A bill is computed with the mode in force when it is computed: it does not keep the mode it opened with, the way it keeps its fee rules. The cloud writes the setting on the store's Tenant layer, beside the country's fields the locale publish writes on its Store layer, and the store receives both; a `locale` node carrying nothing but the setting is not a published locale, so a menu is still refused to a store whose locale was never published.*
- **A fee is a published rule** ([ADR-0159](adr/0159-a-fee-is-configuration.md)). The `fees` configuration node lists any number of fees, each with a `fee_id`, a `code` for reports and a `display_name` with per-locale translations. A fee is a `rate` of its base (`FEE_KIND_PERCENT`), or an integer `amount` per bill (`FEE_KIND_AMOUNT_PER_BILL`) or per unit of each line it counts (`FEE_KIND_AMOUNT_PER_UNIT`). It applies on the `channels` it lists, or on every channel when it lists none, and counts every item, an include list or all but an exclude list (`item_scope`, `menu_item_ids`). A percentage's base is after discounts and comps or before them (`base_discounted`), and net of tax or tax-inclusive (`base_tax_inclusive`). A fee is not taxed, taxed the way its lines are (`FEE_TAX_FOLLOW_LINES`: spread across the base's tax classes in proportion, at each class's rate), or taxed at one named `tax_class_id`; and it is `waivable` or not, and `active` or paused. A field left out takes the default the owner confirmed: after discounts, net of tax, `FEE_TAX_FOLLOW_LINES`, not waivable, active. A fee of a kind the store's release does not know applies to nothing, and one with an item scope it does not know counts no item. **A bill keeps the rules it opened with** (decision 3): those in force for it when it opens — active, clear of every fault, and applying on the channel its order came in on — are frozen onto `billing.bill.opened`, so a publish during the meal does not change what the pre-bill showed. Until a bill opens, the check is quoted with the rules a bill would freeze then, and a bill opened again after a void freezes what is in force then. **A split shares a fee per bill** (decision 6): each part keeps the rules its source froze, and charges a percentage or a fee per unit on its own lines; a fee per bill is allocated across the parts in proportion to the nets of the lines it counts on each, so together they charge exactly what the whole bill would have, and a part with no share does not keep it. A merge keeps the rules of the bill the cashier holds and charges a fee per bill once, at the sum of the merged bills' shares capped at its whole, the amount on the bill they were first split from: merging every part of a split back restores it, merging some charges the sum of their shares, and one the holder dropped, its share being nothing, comes back with the parts that carry it. A merge takes only the bills of one order, which are always parts of one bill (below). `billing.bill.settled` records each fee and the tax per class (§18), and the cloud reports the fees by code (§17). **A fee is waived on one bill as an act** (decision 5), never by editing its rule: a rule published `waivable` is removed from one open bill by `billing.fee.waived` — the bill, the fee and a reason from the managed list for `REASON_ACTION_WAIVE_FEE` — under `billing.fee.waive`, which is PIN-flagged, so a role holds it directly or with approval (§9). The bill then charges the fee nothing and taxes nothing for it, and every other figure is what it would be had the bill never frozen the rule. A waive follows the fee's `fee_id`: every part of a split of the bill keeps it waived, and a merge keeps every fee waived on any bill it puts together. A fee the bill does not charge, or whose rule is not waivable, is refused. *The core computes them (`pos_core::billing::assemble`, ADR-0159 decision 2) from the frozen rules: one fee line per rule that applies; a percentage is taken of the lines it counts, after their share of the bill's discount and comps, and never of another fee; each fee rounds once, by the store's tax rounding; and its taxed parts join their classes' bases before each class's tax is rounded, once. That tax is then shared among the parts of the class's base — each fee's part, the service charge when it is taxed there, and the lines after their reductions — in proportion, so the parts sum exactly to it: each share is rounded down and the last part with a base takes what is left, so wherever a class has lines a fee's part takes exactly its share rounded down. A fee's tax is the sum of its parts'. `service_charge` carries the sum of every fee. A percentage whose base is in the other tax posture from the store's prices applies to nothing for now. A waived fee is left out of the bill before anything is computed (`BillInput::waived_fee_ids`), `merge_fee_rules` keeps the waives of every bill it merges, `waivable_fee` refuses a fee the bill does not charge or whose rule is not waivable, and `decide_bill` waives only on an open bill, under `billing.fee.waive` and its PIN; each fee line says whether its rule is `waivable`. The edge waives one with `POST /api/bills/{id}/fees/{fee_id}/waive`, which takes the reason and, where the person needs one, an approver's code and PIN, and answers with the bill as it now stands; a fee that is not waivable answers `409 FEE_NOT_WAIVABLE`, one the bill does not charge, or has waived already, `409 FEE_NOT_ON_BILL`, and a bill that is not open `409 TRANSITION_REFUSED`, each before anybody is asked to approve. A restart folds every waive back from the log, and every part of a split and every merge keeps it. The check reads and the discount's answer mark each fee line `waivable`, and the till offers **Waive** on such a fee on the pay screen and on the check of a table with one bill open ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §3). The edge installs the `fees` node — absent, or an empty list, is no fee, and a node that does not parse leaves the rules the store had — freezes the rules at every bill open, charges them, and lists each fee as `fee_lines` (`fee_id`, `code`, `display_name`, `amount`, `tax`) on the check reads and on the bill `POST /api/bills/{id}/discount` answers with, named in the store's display language where the store's rule translates it. The settle records each fee and the tax per class. **The receipt, its copy and the pre-bill print each fee on its own line, under its name** (decision 4), where the one service-charge line printed their sum before: in the receipt's language (§12), the translation the store's current rule for its `fee_id` carries, else the name the bill froze. A fee that charged nothing prints no line, and a copy prints the fees and the tax the settle recorded (§11.4). The till's check and pay screens list the same fees ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §3), and the total under them is still the edge's `total_due`. A store with no `fees` node charges no fee, and every bill it makes, and every receipt it prints, is unchanged.*
- **The cloud writes each store's fees** ([ADR-0159](adr/0159-a-fee-is-configuration.md) decision 1). A rule is written in the console, or with `/admin/fees`, at the tenant, a brand or one store. For each fee a store runs the rule written at the most specific scope that reaches it: its own, else its brand's, else its tenant's, so a brand's or a store's rule with the fee's `fee_id` replaces the tenant's there. Every store a write reaches is republished in the same request, its rules resolved and written whole as its `fees` node. An include or exclude list may name item categories as well as items. The cloud compiles each category into the active items it holds when it publishes the store, as it compiles the menu, so the edge needs no category model; an item added to a category is counted from the next publish of the fees (`POST /admin/fees/publish`), as an item reaches a store's menu with the menu's publish. An exclude list that compiles to nothing counts every line. The cloud refuses a rule that fails the node's own checks above, that names an item, an item category or a tax class the catalog does not have, or that a store it would take effect at could not apply: an amount in another currency than the store's, or a tax class the store has no rate for on a channel the fee applies on, either of which would fail every bill the fee applies to. It refuses a second fee under a code a store already runs, which would charge the guest twice, and an item list that names nothing on the menu of any store it takes effect at. A store that can no longer apply a rule it was sent, because its currency or tax table changed after, is not published to and keeps the fees it has, and the publish says which rule and why. **A rule is previewed on a sample bill before it is saved** (ADR-0159's accepted consequences), with `POST /admin/fees/preview`: the store's rules with the rule in place, a few lines from its menu priced as the store prices them, and the totals `pos_core::billing::assemble` gives them with the store's tax table, tax posture, tax rounding and cash rounding — each fee with its tax, the tax per class and the total due. A preview writes and publishes nothing. A rule's rate and amount are prices, so the console reads rules and previews them only with `console.reports.revenue`, and a store's configuration read leaves its `fees` node out for any other role; writing one publishes, so it needs `console.config.publish`. *Built so far: all of the above, in the cloud and on the console's Fees screen ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §5).*
- **Tax is per item class, not per store.** Every menu item carries a `tax_class` (food, drink, alcohol, …) and the rate is resolved from a table in the store's locale pack **keyed by sales channel** — the same item can be taxed differently takeaway and dine-in (Japan charges 8% and 10% respectively). The config key is `store.tax.tax_class_rates`. A flat store-level rate is a *special case* of this table, not a different model: Vietnam v1 populates one class at one rate. Both `tax_class` and the channel dimension are in the schema from day one because retrofitting them is a migration across every order line ever written, and the rate in force is captured in the line snapshot (§14.2) rather than looked up later.
- **Split** by item, evenly into N, or by seat. **Merge** before payment. *Built ([ADR-0128](adr/0128-a-bill-splits-and-merges.md)): a bill covers **lines** rather than an order, so `POST /api/bills/{id}/split` partitions them into new bills — at least two parts, none empty, every line in exactly one — and `POST /api/bills/{id}/merge` folds open bills of one order into the one the cashier holds. A bill on another table is refused `409 BILLS_ON_DIFFERENT_TABLES`, and a bill of another order `409 BILLS_ON_DIFFERENT_ORDERS` — two counter orders share no table to refuse them by — each before anything is written: a settle charges a bill's lines from its own order, so another order's would be charged nothing. The source of a split and every absorbed bill are **terminal**, so the same food cannot be charged twice, and each part computes its own tax and rounding. **Each part is paid on its own**, and the table stays `AWAITING_PAYMENT` until the last part is paid; only then does it need cleaning. A part is read by its own id: `GET /api/bills/{id}/check` gives its state, the lines it covers and what they come to. The table's check (`GET /api/tables/{id}/check`) answers with what every open part owes together. `GET /api/orders/live` names every part still open, as `open_bill_ids`, so a till that reloads mid-split finds each one. **On the till**, the pay screen splits by item: pick one guest's lines, split them off, and pay each part in turn (`docs/ui-ux.md` §3). Both need `billing.bill.split` ([ADR-0158](adr/0158-the-till-enforces-each-persons-own-permissions.md)), and neither asks for a PIN: no money is created, forgiven or moved. **Splitting evenly into N is not this** — four equal shares of a seven-line bill correspond to no grouping of those lines, so it is different arithmetic and needs its own record; today it is several payments against one bill, which settling already supports, and the pay screen offers it for two guests up to the store's `tender_keys.split_ways_max`, six unless the store sets its own, at most twelve ([configuration register](configuration.md)). **By seat** is that partition computed from the seat each line carries: one tap on the pay screen, with the table's own lines, ordered for no seat, as a part of their own.*
- **Discount** by percentage or amount, per line or per bill, with a mandatory reason. Above the role's ceiling it requires a manager PIN entered on the same device.
- **Comp** (giveaway) is distinct from discount and from void: a comp still consumes inventory and is recorded as cost. Accounting and fraud analysis treat the three differently.
- **Payment methods** — cash, card, QR/wallet, voucher, gift card (reserved), other — and **several may be combined on one bill**. A payment carries both `tendered` and `applied_to_bill`; change and over-tender live in the gap. Card payments always have an *unknown result* branch, which parks the bill for reconciliation rather than guessing.
- **Tips**: `tip_amount` is stored separately from the sale amount. Tips may be adjusted after the card is captured (`billing.tip.adjusted`), cash tips are declared at shift close, and a distribution report exists. The UI is enabled per locale; the data model always carries it. *Built so far: where the store takes tips, the pay screen and the counter's pad offer **No tip** and up to three tip keys, each a whole percentage of the bill: `tender_keys.first_tip_percent`, `second_tip_percent` and `third_tip_percent`, in that order, 5, 10 and 15 unless the store sets its own ([configuration register](configuration.md), [ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 2). A key set to `0`, or to a percentage an earlier key offers, is not offered, and with no key left neither shows a tip row. The edge sends the keys with the price book as `tip_percents`; a till reading an older edge offers 5, 10 and 15. They live on a `tender_keys` node of their own, apart from the `tender` node's accepted payment methods, so an edge older than the setting ignores them and keeps its keys.*
- **Receipt numbers are gapless while a single store authority is reachable.** The authority is configuration, `store.billing.receipt_number_authority = store_server | device_blocks`: `store_server` (default) owns one counter incremented in the bill transaction; `device_blocks` hands each device a contiguous block for jurisdictions that accept per-device sequences. A device partitioned from the authority under `store_server` cannot settle — it already cannot, the server holds the database — and the UI says so rather than inventing a number ([ADR-0025](adr/0025-receipt-number-authority.md)).
- **The receipt itemises the sale.** One row per line sold — the name, the quantity, the unit price and the extended amount — printed above the totals and summing to the subtotal, because the rows *are* the records the tax classes were folded from ([ADR-0129](adr/0129-a-receipt-itemises-what-was-sold.md)). What prints is the line snapshot (§14.2), so renaming or repricing an item never alters a receipt for a bill already settled, and a voided line does not appear at all. Under each row, `  + name` lists the modifiers chosen, by the names `sales.order_line.added` recorded from the price book the line was priced against ([ADR-0144](adr/0144-a-line-records-the-names-of-its-modifiers.md)); their prices are already in the unit price, and a line recorded before the event carried names prints none. *Not yet printed, and each needs something the catalog or the event does not carry: the unit of measure Decree 123/2020 Art. 10 asks for, and a per-line tax rate column.*
- **A bill is never opened on nothing.** The edge refuses a bill for an order with no unvoided line (`409 NOTHING_TO_BILL`) and writes nothing: such a bill could never settle, and its table would wait for a payment that cannot come. A table with nothing on it is released instead (§2).
- **A bill covers the dishes it opened on, and no dish after it.** A bill names its lines when it opens, so a line added afterwards would be on no bill: the guest would pay the old total and the line would leave with the table, unpaid. Once a bill is open on an order — or any part of it has been paid — the edge refuses a new line from a table or from the counter (`409 BILL_ALREADY_OPEN`) and writes nothing. To order more, void the bill; the next one covers every line. A line already on the bill can still change quantity before it fires, or be voided, and the bill's total follows it.
- **Voiding a bill** before it settles cancels the bill, not the meal: the lines stay on the order, the table goes back to `OCCUPIED`, and the cashier opens another bill. When another part of a split was already paid, the voided part's lines are forgiven with it and the table goes on to `NEEDS_CLEANING`.
- **Void and refund** after settlement require a manager, a reason, and print a void slip. Refunds happen **only at the store that issued the bill**.

## 6. Shifts and cash

Open a shift with a starting float; every transaction attaches to it. **One open shift per cashier device at a time** — the shift a transaction belongs to is never ambiguous. Whether a store sells while no shift is open is the setting `shift.no_shift_selling` ([configuration register](configuration.md)). By default it does, and such a sale belongs to no shift; a store set to `NO_SHIFT_SELLING_REFUSE` refuses to seat a table, start a counter order or take a payment until a shift opens (`OPEN_SHIFT_REQUIRED`). An order a guest or a delivery partner sends is still taken, and its payment waits for a shift. Paid-in and paid-out entries carry reasons, and are recorded against the open shift before its count: the expected drawer is the float, plus the cash taken, plus what was paid in, minus what was paid out ([ADR-0165](adr/0165-cash-paid-in-and-out-is-counted-in-the-drawer-and-a-no-sale-opening-needs-a-manager.md)). A paid out larger than the drawer should hold is not refused, because the refusal would reveal what the drawer holds. **Closing is blind**: the cashier enters the counted amount *before* the system reveals the expected amount, and only then is the variance shown. A store may turn that off with the setting `shift.blind_close` ([ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 2): the edge then sends the expected amount with the shift before the close as well, and the Shift screen shows it beside the count field, read again each time the screen opens; the variance still appears only at the close. The Shift screen fills in the store's `shift.opening_float_minor` as the opening float, in the store currency's minor unit, and the cashier may change it before opening: the shift opens with the float they send. A store that sets neither opens on an empty float field and counts blind, as before. Opening the drawer outside a sale requires a permission with a manager's PIN, and a reason, and is logged. The drawer itself opens only through the USB printer the console marks as having one.

## 7. Pricing and promotions

One model covers happy hours, item and category discounts, combos, vouchers, and manual reductions:

```
Campaign = scope (tenant/brand/store) + schedule (hours, weekdays)
         + conditions (items, categories, minimum bill, sales channel, customer group)
         + action (percentage, amount, combo price, free item)
         + stacking rules (exclusion groups, priority) + quota + optional voucher codes
```

**Evaluation order is deterministic:** item-level → combo → bill-level → voucher → manual. Every applied campaign appears as its own line on the bill.

**Timing:** item and combo rules are evaluated **when the line is added**; bill-level rules and vouchers **when payment begins**. A guest who ordered at 16:59 keeps the happy-hour price even if they pay at 17:30.

**Offline rule of the engine:** *rules run offline, uniqueness runs online.* Happy hours, combos, and manual discounts work with no connection. **Voucher redemption is an atomic check-and-mark against the cloud**, which makes double redemption impossible. With no connection the voucher button is greyed out; everything else still sells.

**Channel price lists** are separate from promotions: dine-in, takeaway, and each marketplace may have different base prices (marketplace prices usually absorb commission).

## 8. Inventory

- **Ingredients** with units (g, ml, piece) and their own categories.
- **Bill of materials per item *and* per modifier** — a large pizza is the base recipe plus 50 g of dough.
- **Stock is deducted when the line is fired**, because that is when the kitchen consumes it. Cancelling a fired line records **waste**, it does not return stock (configurable).
- **Available-to-make**, recalculated in microseconds and pushed to every screen in under 50 ms:

```
available(item) = floor( min over ingredients ( stock[i] ÷ recipe[item][i] ) )
```

Shared ingredients propagate correctly: if A needs D and E, and B needs C and D, cooking A lowers B's availability because D was consumed. Hitting the threshold triggers **auto-86** plus a notification to marketplaces. The cloud builds the same projection from consumption events (1–3 s behind) for brand-level views.

- Ledger entries: consumption (automatic), receipt, adjustment, waste, **stocktake** (records `counted_qty` and `count_time`; the delta is computed against the projection *at the moment of counting*, so sales during the count do not corrupt it).
- Two modes per tenant: **ERP-led** (the ERP owns master data and purchasing, the POS emits consumption) and **standalone** (the framework provides manual receipts, stocktakes, waste, and thresholds).
- Availability numbers are theoretical; spillage makes them drift. Stocktaking is what pulls them back to reality. The two belong together.

## 9. Permissions (RBAC)

Permissions are a fixed catalogue owned by the framework. **Roles are data**: sets of permissions plus parameters, edited in the cloud, synced to stores, evaluated at the edge so offline behaves identically. Any permission may be flagged "requires PIN on the spot".

Groups: sales · billing · cash and shifts · reports · menu and inventory · store administration · cloud-only administration. Role parameters include discount ceilings (percentage and amount) and price-override ceilings. *Built so far: a role carries an **amount** ceiling, authored in the console, compiled onto each assigned person in the `permissions` node and read by the edge. An unpublished ceiling is **zero**, not unlimited — so a role with none set needs `billing.discount.override_ceiling` and a manager's PIN for any discount, and the day a tenant sets one a server's small discount goes through with no code change. A manager's approval sent with a discount within the ceiling approves nothing: it is not checked, and no override is recorded. A **percentage** ceiling and the **price-override** ceiling are not built; each needs its own field and its own comparison.* The `permissions` node is one type, `pos_proto::people::PublishedPermissions`: the cloud's compiler writes it and the edge reads it, so neither can drop a field the other added ([ADR-0158](adr/0158-the-till-enforces-each-persons-own-permissions.md) decision 10). Every role that existed before ADR-0158 named the seven till acts — `sales.table.manage`, `sales.line.add`, `sales.line.fire`, `sales.ticket.bump`, `billing.bill.open`, `billing.bill.split` and `billing.payment.take` — holds all seven, granted once by migration `0073`: a role keeps every act it had, and a later boot does not hand back a permission an owner has since removed. Every role that existed before `billing.fee.waive` holds it on the terms it holds `billing.discount.override_ceiling`, granted once by migration `0076` ([ADR-0159](adr/0159-a-fee-is-configuration.md) decision 5): directly where it granted the override directly, so its holders approve a waive for others, and with approval otherwise; nothing else in either list changes. Every role that existed before `reports.takings.view` and granted both `cash.shift.close` and a PIN-flagged permission directly, an approver who closes shifts, holds it directly, granted once by migration `0079` ([ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 2). Applied to a new tenant's starting roles, the same rule picks exactly the roles that hold it out of the box, the supervisor, the manager and the owner, so existing and new tenants agree; no other role was given it, and a cashier sees takings only where an owner grants it in the console. Per person it lists the permissions they hold **directly**, which they act on alone, and those they hold **with approval** (`permissions_with_approval`), which another person holding them directly authorises one act at a time with their code and PIN (decision 4); the node also carries the store's rollout switch, `enforced`. *Built so far: the edge reads both lists and the switch. Where `enforced` is true it decides each command with the acting person's own set (decision 1): what they hold directly they do alone, with nobody's PIN even for a PIN-flagged permission; what they hold with approval needs the code and PIN of somebody else who holds it directly, never their own (decision 5); and what they hold neither way is refused without asking for an approver. An approver is taken only by the acts that ask for one today — voiding a fired line or a bill, a discount above the ceiling, waiving a fee, and opening the drawer without a sale, the PIN-flagged permissions a route checks — so any other permission is usable only when held directly. A person no roster lists holds nothing. `GET /api/session` reports the switch and the signed-in person's two lists, sorted (`permissions_enforced`, `permissions`, `permissions_with_approval`); where the store enforces, the till hides what they may not do and asks for an approver only where they hold the act with approval (decision 6, [`ui-ux.md`](ui-ux.md) §3), and the edge stays the authority. Where `enforced` is false or absent the edge decides with the store-wide set, as before, and a manager alone on shift still approves their own act. `enforced` is the setting `permissions.enforced` ([configuration register](configuration.md)): off for every store that existed before it, and on for a store the console creates after it, whose roles must therefore hold what its staff need before it opens. The cloud writes it on the store's Tenant layer, and the merge puts it beside the staff the people compiler writes on the Store layer. A `permissions` node with no `staff` key, which is what a store with no people node receives, sets the switch and keeps the roster the edge holds; a `staff` list that is present, even empty, replaces the roster.*

**What the console authors is what the store enforces** ([ADR-0158](adr/0158-the-till-enforces-each-persons-own-permissions.md) decisions 3 and 7). An assignment gives a person a role at one store, at every store of a store group ([ADR-0122](adr/0122-a-store-group-is-a-delivery-cohort.md)), whichever stores the group holds at the time, or at every store of the tenant, one opened later included. A person holds at most one assignment per store, one per group and one tenant-wide, and a store's node lists everyone whose assignments reach it, each once, holding the union of their roles' permissions there and the highest of their ceilings. An archived role grants nothing — no permission and no ceiling, exactly as a role that does not exist — and a person whose every role is archived stays on the roster holding nothing; archiving the person, or removing the assignment, is what takes them off. Removing an assignment, archiving a person, archiving a role and creating an assignment to a group or to every store publish the `permissions` node to every store the change reaches before the request answers, the same publish as **Publish** and audited the same way, by version and staff count with the id of what was removed, archived or assigned; the answer lists each store and whether its publish applied, and a store whose publish failed is published again by hand. Adding a store to a group an assignment names, or taking it out, publishes to that store the same way, and the membership answer lists it. A group or the tenant reaches only its open stores, so an archived store is not published to; an archived group still reaches the stores it holds. Every other change to people, roles or assignments, a new assignment to one store included, reaches a store when someone publishes. An assignment names exactly one place: `store_id`, `store_group_id`, or `scope_kind` `ASSIGNMENT_SCOPE_TENANT` for every store, and anything else is refused `400`. It is refused for a person, store, group or role the tenant does not have (`404`), another tenant's group included, or has archived (`409`). A second assignment of a person at a place they already hold one at is refused `409` `ALREADY_EXISTS`, naming `store_id`, `store_group_id` or `scope_kind`, and writes, records and publishes nothing: the assignment that is there is changed or removed instead. `GET /admin/assignments` lists everyone who works at a store, an assignment of any scope that reaches it, by `store_id`, and also lists by `employee_id`, by `store_group_id`, or every tenant-wide assignment by `scope_kind`; each row says its `scope_kind` beside the `store_id` or `store_group_id` it names.

**A role says how it grants each permission** ([ADR-0158](adr/0158-the-till-enforces-each-persons-own-permissions.md) decision 4). A role grants each permission either directly or with approval, and only a PIN-flagged permission can be granted with approval. A role's `permissions` are what it grants directly and its `permissions_with_approval` what it grants with approval, never one in both. Only a person who holds a permission directly approves it for somebody else, so the holders of a permission granted with approval cannot approve it, at any store, whether or not it enforces each person's own set. The compiler gives each person the union of their active roles' with-approval grants, less everything they hold directly: of two roles that disagree, the one that lets them act alone wins. An archived role contributes neither list. In the console's role editor, every PIN-flagged permission a role grants shows a choice, **With approval** or **Directly**, and one newly ticked starts with approval: the editor is where the catalogue's PIN flag is the default a new role starts from, and it always sends both lists. `POST /admin/roles` and `PATCH /admin/roles/{role_id}` take both lists; an id in `permissions_with_approval` that is unknown, not PIN-flagged or also in `permissions` is refused `400`. A request that leaves the field out works as before the list existed: a create grants every permission it lists directly and reads back as it was sent, and an update keeps what the role grants with approval, less anything it now grants directly. Every role that existed before migration `0074` keeps its direct list byte for byte and was given, once, each PIN-flagged permission it did not grant directly, with approval, so its holders can do with somebody's approval what they could before; a role that granted one directly, such as a manager's void, still does, so its holders still approve it for others. *Built so far: a store that does not enforce decides as before, with the direct list alone, which is also the list an approver must hold. `GET /api/session` reports the with-approval list beside the direct one, and the till ignores both until the store enforces. Three PIN-flagged permissions no route checks yet, `billing.comp.apply`, `billing.price.override` and `billing.refund.issue`, can be granted with approval and do nothing until a route asks for an approver for them.*

**A new tenant starts with six roles** ([ADR-0158](adr/0158-the-till-enforces-each-persons-own-permissions.md), Rollout). A tenant the console creates gets one editable role for each of `pos-core`'s built-in roles, owner, manager, supervisor, cashier, server and cook, granting what the catalogue's `default_roles` say: a permission the role is a default for directly, a PIN-flagged one it is not a default for with approval, and nothing else. None has a discount ceiling. Each is named in the language the console creating the tenant is shown in, from the console's own language packs (`people.startingRole.*`), and in English where they carry no such language; the tenant renames, changes or archives them like any role. The seeding is audited as one `role.seed` entry with the ids and counts, and it is idempotent: each role's id is derived from the tenant's, so seeding a tenant again writes nothing. A tenant that existed before is not touched.

**Before a store enforces, the console says what each role lacks** ([ADR-0158](adr/0158-the-till-enforces-each-persons-own-permissions.md), Rollout). `GET /admin/stores/{store_id}/permissions/readiness` lists each active role an active person holds at the store, through an assignment of any scope that reaches it, with how many people hold it, each once, and every permission a store decides that the role grants neither directly nor with approval, with its group and risk: low risk first, then medium, then high, and catalogue order within a level. The cloud administration permissions are left out, because no till asks for them. It also counts the people at the store who hold no active role, who can do nothing once the store enforces, and says what the store runs for `permissions.enforced` and which level sets it. It needs `console.people.read`, names roles and counts people, and names no person. It compares roles with the catalogue and nothing else: what anybody did at the till is not read, because guessing what a person needs from what they did would be monitoring staff. No migration, event or protocol change.

**Adding a permission must not be manual work.** Each permission is a declaration in `pos-core` carrying id (`domain.resource.action`), group, description, risk level, default roles, and PIN flag. **Deny by default.** Adding one makes the compiler force template updates, makes the dashboard show it automatically, tags the audit log, and regenerates the documented matrix. Removing a permission is forbidden — deprecate only — and CI keeps a snapshot so any change is a visible diff. Enforcement goes through a single `require(permission)` function; ad-hoc role string checks are banned.

**Every state-changing till route names its permission** ([ADR-0158](adr/0158-the-till-enforces-each-persons-own-permissions.md) decision 2). Seating, clearing and releasing a table need `sales.table.manage`; adding a line, changing its quantity, voiding one that never fired and starting a counter order need `sales.line.add`; firing a line, a course or the order, and holding or resuming a line, need `sales.line.fire`; bumping a ticket needs `sales.ticket.bump`; opening a bill and printing a pre-bill need `billing.bill.open`; splitting and merging need `billing.bill.split`; settling needs `billing.payment.take`; waiving a fee needs `billing.fee.waive`; opening a shift needs `cash.shift.open`. A transfer needs `sales.order.transfer` and, for the table it seats, `sales.table.manage`. The table of every `POST` route and what it needs is `ROUTE_PERMISSIONS` in `crates/pos-edge/src/http/route_permissions.rs`; a test holds it to `docs/snapshots/routes.txt`, so a new route that names nothing fails CI, and every catalogue permission is either checked by a route or listed there as not yet on one. *Where a store does not enforce each person's own set (ADR-0158 decision 1) — every store until the console writes `enforced` — the edge decides with a store-wide set that grants every permission, so these checks refuse nobody there: they are named so that turning enforcement on is a change of set, not of code.* Retiring a device (`POST /api/pair/revoke`) needs a signed-in person whose own role grants `admin.device.manage`, as minting a pairing code does (decision 8), whether or not the store enforces each person's own set. Seeing the day's takings (`GET /api/reports/takings`, §17) likewise needs a signed-in person whose own role grants `reports.takings.view`, in every store: takings are confidential, and no till showed them before, so a store that does not enforce yet has nothing there to keep. Seeing which of the store's terminals this device or another holds (`GET /api/print/agent`, the Devices screen's **This device** card) needs a signed-in person whose own role grants `admin.device.manage`, as binding and releasing one do ([ADR-0112](adr/0112-print-agents.md)); it names no other device. They are the two reads in `ROUTE_PERMISSIONS`, and `403 PERMISSION_DENIED` answers anyone else. POS Station asks whether its token is still accepted with `GET /api/pair/this_device`, behind the paired-device gate alone because nobody need be signed in. It answers for the calling token only, with `accepted`, the caller's own `device_id` and its `pair_time`, and names no other device. The device list (`GET /api/pair/devices`) also stays behind the paired-device gate alone, for as long as a Station older than that route still reads the list as its probe.

## 10. Store profiles (capability flags)

A profile is a set of flags in the configuration tree — `tables_enabled`, `tabs_enabled`, `seats_enabled`, `kds_enabled`, `courses_enabled`, `pay_first_enabled`, `barcode_enabled`, `queue_number_enabled`, `tips_enabled`, `qr_ordering_enabled` — not three separate applications. `qr_ordering_enabled` is QR ordering's one switch (§13).

| | Full-service | Cafe / counter | Retail *(data model day 1, UI phase 2)* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start screen | Floor plan | Order at counter | Barcode field |
| Payment | After service, splits | **Before service**, queue number | At the counter |
| Kitchen | Multi-station KDS + courses | Single station or printer only | None |
| Inventory | Recipe-based | Simple recipes | 1:1 by SKU |

Items carry `sku`, `barcode`, and variant fields from day one so enabling retail later needs no migration. Flags are read through a single `CapabilityContext`; scattering `if flag` through the code is banned. Validity rules between flags (for example, `pay_first_enabled` implies tables are off) are validated in the cloud before a config version is published.

**The console offers a switch only where a release reads it** ([ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 5). `GET /admin/capabilities` marks each flag `offered`. Four are not: `tabs_enabled`, `pay_first_enabled`, `barcode_enabled` and `queue_number_enabled`. No tab, no pay-first flow and no barcode field exists, and queue numbers are always issued: every tableless order is given the day's next number whatever `queue_number_enabled` says. So nothing in the edge, the domain or the till reads the four. The Config screen leaves them out of its switches and of what a preset or a publish sets, and does not offer the Retail preset, which names only barcode entry and so would only turn every switch off. A store that has one on is shown it as set but not used by this release. A store's stored value is kept, sent and read into its capability set as before, and the cloud still refuses `pay_first_enabled` with `tables_enabled`. So that table service stays reachable, a publish that turns tables on for a store whose stored `pay_first_enabled` is on turns that off too, and so does a store-group copy that turns tables on. A flag is offered again in the change that gives it a reader. QR ordering's switch is offered on Channels & payments (§13).

**A walk-in's channel is the store's to choose.** The counter opens an order for a walk-in guest ([ADR-0146](adr/0146-a-counter-store-starts-its-own-orders.md)) on the setting `counter.walk_in_channel` ([configuration register](configuration.md), [ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 2), and the channel decides the price book the order is sold from and its tax (§5): in Japan a meal eaten in is taxed at 10% and one taken away at 8%. `WALK_IN_CHANNEL_TAKEAWAY` is the default and what every counter did before the setting; `WALK_IN_CHANNEL_DINE_IN` opens every walk-in eaten in; under `WALK_IN_CHANNEL_ASK` the till names the guest's choice. A channel the till names in `POST /api/orders` wins, and a till that names none gets the store's channel, which is takeaway under ask. A store that publishes the channels it accepts must accept the channel a walk-in takes, or the edge refuses the order (`CHANNEL_NOT_ACCEPTED`). The setting sits on a `counter` node of its own rather than on `channels`, because an edge from before it reads a `channels` node without its list as a store that accepts no channel.

## 11. Fraud controls

1. **Blind shift close** (§6) — removes "count until it matches". A store that turns `shift.blind_close` off gives this control up for its own tills.
2. **Mandatory reasons** from a cloud-managed list for: voiding fired lines, discounts, comps, refunds, bill voids, opening the drawer outside a sale.
3. **Per-employee analytics** on the dashboard: void, discount, refund, drawer-open, and reprint rates against peers, with automatic outlier flags.
4. **Reprints** are marked COPY, counted, and permissioned ([ADR-0164](adr/0164-a-receipt-is-reprinted-as-a-marked-copy-and-every-reprint-is-counted.md)). A copy is the settled receipt under its own number, headed **COPY** and "Reprint *n* - *time*", printed from the figures `billing.bill.settled` recorded: each fee under its name and the tax per class as the settle recorded them (`fee_lines` and `tax_lines`, §18), whatever the rates and the fees in force say now. A settle recorded before it carried its tax per class prints as before: the copy computes the lines again, and when the rates in force no longer give the recorded tax, it prints that tax as one total. How the reductions divide between a discount and a comp, and the named parts of a tax line, are not recorded, so a copy takes them from the bill computed again where they add up to what was recorded; otherwise it prints the reductions as one line and the tax line without its parts. Every press writes `billing.receipt.reprinted` with its copy number, under `billing.receipt.reprint`, which asks no PIN. The till copies a bill settled in the current business day, from the Today screen's recent bills (`GET /api/bills/settled`, newest first, at most 200) or **Print again** on the pay screens, through `POST /api/bills/{id}/receipt/reprint`; a bill that has not settled has nothing to copy (`409 NOT_SETTLED`), and an earlier day's receipt is refused (`409 RECEIPT_FROM_ANOTHER_DAY`). **Price override** is a separate permission with its own ceiling.
5. Immutable audit log plus NTP-synchronised clocks, so records line up with store camera footage where it exists.
6. **No training mode.** "Sell without recording" is a classic fraud vector. Training happens in a demo store with a sample menu.
7. **A manager's PIN is limited like a sign-in PIN.** A wrong PIN at an approval prompt counts against that manager exactly as a wrong one at sign-in does, in one count per person: five in a row lock both for five minutes, and a right PIN clears it (ADR-0030).

## 12. Internationalisation

**Two tiers of strings.** *Framework UI strings* (buttons, labels, errors) are embedded in the binary, shipped as language packs with releases, and may be overridden per tenant. *Tenant content* (item names, categories, modifiers, campaign names, receipt templates) lives in the configuration tree as `{"en": required, "vi": ..., "ja": ...}`, syncs like any config, and works offline.

**English is always present and is the fallback**, so no screen ever shows a blank or a raw key; missing translations count toward a completion percentage shown to admins.

**Resolution order:** employee → device → store → brand → tenant → `en`. **Guest language is separate from staff language** — staff may work in Vietnamese while receipts print in Japanese. Kitchen tickets follow the *station's* language.

**Standards:** keys are `domain.screen.element`, append-only; messages use **ICU MessageFormat** for interpolation and plurals; dates, numbers, and currency follow the store's locale pack. CI blocks hardcoded user-visible strings.

**Thermal printer caveat:** most ESC/POS printers lack full Unicode fonts, so Vietnamese diacritics and CJK print as garbage. Any line containing characters outside the printer's code page is **rendered as a bitmap** before printing — a few milliseconds slower, correct on every model. Such a line is drawn at the store's `printing.font_size_dots` ([configuration.md](configuration.md), [ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 6): 24 printer dots per em unless the store sets 16 to 48, or the store server's own deprecated `font_size_dots` while the store sets none, and twice that for double-size text. A change applies from the next print. A line the printer's own character set covers prints in the printer's font, which the setting does not change.

**Each printer's paper.** The console's Devices page says what paper each printer takes and whether it cuts it, and the store hears it on the `devices` node once its devices are published ([ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 2): **80 mm** paper at 42 characters a line, **80 mm at 48 characters**, or **58 mm** at 32. A receipt, its copy and a pre-bill decide from that width whether a bilingual label shares its line, and every line a printer is sent as a bitmap, on any document, is drawn to its paper: 576 dots wide on 80 mm and 384 on 58 mm, so a 58 mm printer is never sent an image too wide for its head. A printer the console says has no cutter is sent no cut, and its paper is torn off by hand. A printer nobody has set is the 80 mm printer with a cutter that every printer was taken to be, so a store that sets nothing prints exactly what it printed before. The code page is not a choice: the edge sends text only in ASCII, which every ESC/POS printer has, and draws every other line.

**The words on paper.** The fixed labels a receipt, a pre-bill and a shift report print ("Subtotal", "Tax", "PRE-BILL", "Variance"…) come from a table per language in the edge (`crates/pos-edge/src/paper_labels.rs`) — Vietnamese and English today. A receipt, its copy and a pre-bill print in the **receipt's language**, the `printing` node's `receipt_language` ([configuration.md](configuration.md), [ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md)): the store's display language unless the store chooses otherwise, which is what every receipt printed in before; the language of the store's country, which a new store is given (Vietnamese in Vietnam); or Vietnamese or English by name. The country's language is the country pack's, which the cloud publishes on the store's `locale` node as `country_language`; until the node names one the edge has labels in, such a receipt prints in the display language. In a language other than the display language each item and modifier prints the menu's translation for it, and the name it was rung up with where the menu has none. A fee prints its rule's translation in the receipt's language whichever language that is, because the name a bill freezes is the rule's own rather than one in the display language; where the store's current rule for the fee has no translation for it, or the store no longer runs the rule, the fee prints the name the bill froze ([ADR-0159](adr/0159-a-fee-is-configuration.md)). **A receipt may print a second language**, the `printing` node's `receipt_second_language`: none by default and for a new store, because a bilingual receipt is each store's choice, or Vietnamese or English. Each label of a receipt, its copy and a pre-bill then prints in the receipt's language and then in the second, as `Tạm tính / Subtotal`, on one line where the two fit the printer's width, and otherwise with the second on the next line, indented two spaces; a heading centres the second under the first. An item, a modifier and a fee print their name in the second language on the next line, indented, where the menu or the fee's rule translates it, or where the second language is the one the item was rung up in, and only where that name differs from the one above it. A second language that is the one the labels already print in, or one a store PC with no fonts cannot draw, prints the receipt in one language, as before. Whether the two fit on one line is a question of the printer's paper, above: 42 characters for a printer nobody has set. The shift report keeps the display language. **A kitchen ticket prints in its station's language**, the station's `ticket_language` on the `stations` node ([ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 2), with the receipt language's four choices, resolved as a receipt's is, and the display language for a station that sets none, which is what every ticket printed in before. Its labels and each item's and modifier's name, where the menu translates it, are in that language. It is the language of the station whose printer prints the ticket, so a ticket that goes to a backup station's printer prints in the backup's (§4). Paper only: the kitchen display keeps the language its device shows. When the store PC has no fonts to rasterise with, the labels fall back to English, because a label must never be the line that stops a receipt printing. Printing in the *guest's* language rather than the store's is not built yet.

**A till's own receipts.** A terminal on the `devices` node can name the printer its receipts, receipt copies and pre-bills go to, `receipt_printer_id`, and the languages they print in, `receipt_language` and `receipt_second_language`, each in place of the store's ([ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 4). The console's Devices page sets them. A paired device is that till once a manager binds it to the terminal on the till's **Devices → This device**, the print-agent binding of [ADR-0112](adr/0112-print-agents.md), and needs no agent installed to be one: for a request from that device the edge prints the receipt, its copy or the pre-bill at the terminal's printer, in its languages, resolved as the store's are, with the same choices and a second language of none as a choice. A till whose terminal names nothing, a device bound to no terminal, and a printer the node does not list as one serving no station print at the store's receipt printer, in the store's languages, as every till did before. A till's printer that does not answer is reported as any printer's failure, and the edge does not try the store's: a bill printed at the wrong counter is worse than one that visibly did not print. A till the edge cannot tell, because the binding could not be read while a terminal names its own, is refused for the same reason. Kitchen tickets, the shift report and the cash drawer stay the store's, so a cash payment at the bar till opens the store's drawer until the multi-drawer shift work gives each till its own.

Cloud screen: a translation grid (English ↔ target), a "missing only" filter, completion percentage, and CSV import/export.

## 13. QR ordering (cloud module)

Guests scan a QR code on the table, open a web app served by the **cloud**, pick a language, browse the menu at `QR` channel prices, and submit. The cloud validates and publishes the order over NATS; the edge receives it through the same `OrderIn` port used by marketplaces. If the table already has an open order, the items join it where the store sets `qr.table_order` to `TABLE_ORDER_JOIN`, each keeping the `QR` order it was ordered on, and stay an order of their own where it does not.

**A guest's order and the table's** ([ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) item 2, the `qr.table_order` setting in [configuration.md](configuration.md)). With `TABLE_ORDER_JOIN`, which a new store is given, a guest's lines join the table's open order, so the table has one order, one bill and one send to the kitchen: a joined line is fired, voided, billed and split like any line a waiter adds, and goes to the kitchen with the table's next send. With `TABLE_ORDER_SEPARATE`, the default and what every store did before the setting, the guest's order is an order of its own on the table, as it always was.
- **When it joins.** As it arrives where nobody has to confirm a guest's order. Where staff confirm them, it is its own order while it waits, which the kitchen cannot see, and joins when staff confirm it; refused, it never joined, and the table's order is as it was. A guest's order the hold was holding when the store turned the hold off is released as its own order and does not join.
- **What it joins.** The order the table holds, or, where a waiting guest's order took the table over, the newest other order still owing at the table, which is the order a refusal would give the table back to. Two guests' orders at a table nobody seated become one: the first is the table's order and the next joins it.
- **When it stays its own.** A table whose order cannot take a line keeps the guest's order separate, as before: its bill is open, so a line rung now would be on no bill (§5), it has been split into parts, or it has been paid. So does a waiting guest's order whose table asked for the bill before staff confirmed it.
- **What the log says.** The guest's order is opened as it always was, on the `QR` channel, with its lines, and `sales.table.merged` then names the table's order as `target_order_id` and the guest's as `merged_order_id`, in the same transaction as the lines or as the confirmation. So each line keeps the order, and through it the channel, it was ordered on, and a guest's submission keeps an order of its own: a retry is answered with that order's id and `created: false`. The folded order ends there: it has no line left, takes none, and never waits for staff. A bill names the lines it covers, so a bill read from the log needs nothing from the merge.
- **What a joined line costs.** The price the guest was quoted, from the `QR` book. The bill taxes it and charges fees as it does every line on the table's order, at that order's channel, which is the dining room's for a seated table, and not at the `QR` channel's rates.
- **On screen.** No till screen, kitchen board or ticket shows which channel a line came from, so a joined line looks like the waiter's. The till folds `sales.table.merged` from its live link; a till page loaded before its edge updated shows the guest's lines apart until it next reloads the open orders.

**One switch** ([ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 5). Whether a store takes QR orders is the capability flag `qr_ordering_enabled` (§10), off unless the store's configuration turns it on. While it is off the cloud's guest page refuses a guest's order (`404`). The edge refuses an order on the `QR` channel however it arrived, relayed or through `POST /v1/orders` (`FAILED_PRECONDITION`, naming the flag), where the store's configuration switches QR ordering off: the switch published `false`, with `qr.enabled` `false` beside it, as the cloud publishes it. A configuration that does not carry the switch decides by the channel list alone, as the edge did before the switch, and so does a `false` beside a `qr.enabled` that is not `false`, which is the flag as it stood before the switch, written with every other flag by the console's capability form while nothing read it. So an edge may be updated before its cloud. The two values that used to decide it follow the switch, so an edge on an earlier release, which reads only the channel list, agrees with it: every publish of the switch, of the `qr` node or of the `channels` list sets `qr.enabled` to the switch, and puts the `QR` channel in a published list while the switch is on and takes it out while it is off. What a list means does not change. A store with no list takes every channel, so nothing is added for it; an empty list stays empty; and a list holding only `QR` keeps it, since an empty one would refuse every order. A `qr` publish that sends `enabled` writes it as the switch, for an older console and a cohort copy made by one. The console offers the switch on Channels & payments, beside the guardrails. A rollback to a version published before the switch existed gives that version the switch the store then ran. The raw layer editor (`PUT /admin/stores/{id}/config/{level}`) writes what it is given, and nothing follows a switch written there.

**Deliberate boundaries.** Payment in v1 happens **at the counter** — online payment gateways are a future adapter group. QR ordering is a **cloud feature and is not offline-first**: if the store or the cloud is unreachable, the page tells the guest to call a staff member. Staff are always the fallback, and no end-customer SLA is promised.

**A refused guest order leaves the floor** ([ADR-0116](adr/0116-the-qr-hold-is-derived-and-it-gates-firing.md) decision 5): it drops off every till's live list, and its table goes back to the order it held before the guest's arrived, or is freed if there was none.

**Abuse controls for a static printed QR code** (which can be photographed and used from outside): **staff confirmation before firing is on by default**, plus per-table rate limits, orders accepted only during opening hours, and only while the store is online. An optional guest phone number is PII and follows §15.

## 14. Data-correctness laws

These are invariants, not preferences. Property tests are written against them.

1. **Business date.** Each store has a configurable day-cutoff hour (default 04:00 local; a daytime shop may set 00:00). Every event is stamped with `business_date`; rollups, reports, and shift closes use it, so a bill rung at 01:30 belongs to the previous evening. The country module uses the **calendar date** for legal invoices. Two concepts, two fields, never mixed.
2. **Line snapshots.** A line captures price, tax class and rate, display name, and promotion outcome **at the moment it is added**. It never references the live menu. Changing or deleting a menu item does not alter open orders.
3. **Split rounding.** The parts of a split must sum **exactly** to the original total; the rounding remainder goes to the last part, and bill-level discounts are allocated proportionally. CI asserts `sum(splits) == original_total`.
4. **Exclusive settlement.** `bill:settle` is a one-time state transition. A second attempt returns `FAILED_PRECONDITION`. The UI takes a soft lock when a payment screen opens and tells other devices who is paying.
5. **Payment sum.** For a bill in `SETTLED`, `sum(payment.applied_to_bill) == bill.total_due`, and `sum(tendered) − sum(applied_to_bill) − sum(tip) == change_given`. Tips are a separate ledger, never part of `total_due`. CI asserts both identities and that the printed per-tax-class lines reconcile to the printed tax total ([ADR-0028](adr/0028-settlement-and-payment-invariant.md)).
6. **Merge convergence.** Same-line edits merge commutatively and associatively, terminal states winning, so two devices reach one state regardless of sync order and a voided line is never resurrected ([ADR-0029](adr/0029-append-command-merge-semantics.md)).

## 15. Tenant lifecycle and personal data

The framework provides **mechanism**; the operator sets **policy**.

Three platform operations: **Suspend** (service off, data retained, stores read-only), **Export** (the tenant's full data as a downloadable package), **Delete** (anonymise personal data and remove operational data while retaining financial records).

The framework hardcodes **no retention period** and makes no legal determination — retention is configuration, defaulting per country locale pack, because the operator is the data controller. What the framework does guarantee technically: **after anonymisation, financial figures still reconcile**.

**Mandatory design consequence:** the event log is immutable, so **PII is never embedded in event payloads**. Personal data lives in a separate record keyed by `subject_id`; events carry only the id. Anonymising is deleting one record — the log is never rewritten. (Equivalent alternative: encrypt per subject and destroy the key.) Without this decision, "delete one customer's data" later means rewriting all history and every backup.

## 16. Other operational rules

- **Employee PINs**: minimum length configurable, unique within a store, locked out after a number of wrong PINs in a row for a number of minutes (enforced offline too), every failure audited. By default five wrong PINs lock a person out for five minutes. A store sets its own numbers with `session.lockout_attempts` (3 to 10) and `session.lockout_minutes` (1 to 60) ([configuration register](configuration.md)), so no value switches the lockout off. Device activation codes and setup tokens are rate-limited and expire after repeated failures.
- **Queue numbers reset daily**, on the store's business date. They are a customer-facing call number for counter service and are a different counter from the store-lifetime, gapless receipt number (§5) — the two must never share an implementation.
- **Bulk menu import** (CSV/Excel with preview, per-row errors, and column mapping) at brand creation — required for chains migrating from another POS.
- **Employees belong to a tenant; roles are granted per store.** Working at two stores means two grants, one `employee_id`, one PIN.
- **Device screen lock** after `session.idle_lock_seconds` with no touch, unlocked by PIN ([configuration register](configuration.md)). The till signs its person out on the store server and covers the screen, and their PIN opens it again where they were. `0`, the default, never locks, and a new store is given two minutes. The kitchen board and the pass never lock.
- **Buyer details** on a bill (`buyer_name`, `buyer_tax_code`, `buyer_email`) are optional PII feeding the country module's corporate-invoice flow.
- **Marketplace order containing a just-86'd item**: configurable per vendor — reject the whole order, or accept without the item and notify.
- **Menu scheduling (dayparts)**: items and categories may be restricted to time windows and weekdays — this controls *whether an item is sold at all*, unlike happy-hour pricing which only changes the price. Configured per brand, overridable per store.
- **Online order throttling**: a configurable ceiling on marketplace and QR orders accepted per 15-minute window, with the resulting prep time published back to the vendor. Protects the kitchen during peaks instead of silently accumulating late orders.
- **Customer-facing display (CFD)**: an optional device role showing the running order and total to the guest at the counter. It is a device type in the existing device model, paired and revoked like any other.
- **Time clock (light)**: employees clock in and out with their existing PIN; the system reports hours worked per employee and per shift. Payroll is explicitly out of scope — this exists so labour hours can be compared with sales in reporting.
- **Receipt customisation**: logo, footer text, and a promotional line, configured per brand and overridable per store; rendered as bitmap where the printer lacks the character set (§12).

## 17. Reporting

At the store, one **Today** screen: revenue, bill count, average bill, revenue by payment method and by hour, top items, total discounts and voids with the underlying list. *Built so far: the **Takings today** tile, which is the bills settled in the current business day at what their guests paid, and how many, read from `GET /api/reports/takings` by a person whose own role grants `reports.takings.view` (§9, [ADR-0160](adr/0160-everything-a-store-runs-differently-is-published-configuration.md) decision 2). It is the store's figure and nothing finer: no person, till or shift, because takings per person would be monitoring staff, which needs a legal basis the till does not have. The tile is hidden from anyone else.*

In the cloud: day, month, and **custom date ranges**, comparisons across stores and brands, filtering by customer group, and **CSV export** on every report.

**Fees are reported by code** ([ADR-0159](adr/0159-a-fee-is-configuration.md) decision 4). The cloud's revenue rollup totals each trading day's fees from the `fee_lines` of its settled bills, by the fee's `code`: the name the latest bill recorded, the bills that charged it, what it charged and the tax on it. The day's `service_charge` stays the sum of every fee. A bill settled by an edge from before fee lines adds to `service_charge` and to no code, and nothing stands in for the difference; a line that charged nothing counts no bill. What a waive forgave is not reported, because `billing.fee.waived` carries no amount. *Built: the rollup's `by_fee`, which a rollup stored before it gains from its cursor forward and a reset of the rollup ([ADR-0036](adr/0036-materialised-rollups.md)) re-folds from the log; `GET /admin/stores/{store_id}/revenue/fees/export`, which downloads `revenue-fees.csv` with one row per day per code — `business_date,currency_code,fee_code,fee_name,bills,amount,tax` — over the revenue export's window and behind its permission, `console.reports.revenue`, while `revenue.csv` keeps its columns; and the **Fees** card on the console's Reports screen ([ui-ux.md](ui-ux.md) §5).*

**Customer groups** (staff, VIP, members) exist for exactly two purposes: as a condition in the pricing engine, and as a reporting dimension. They are explicitly **not** a CRM — no per-customer purchase history, no points, no profiles.

**PDF menu generation** (cloud): pick brand, store, and language, and export a printable menu or a **view-only** QR page. Ordering from that page is QR ordering (§13); the PDF path stays view-only.

## 18. Event catalogue

Names follow `domain.resource.action` ([naming-and-api.md](naming-and-api.md) §5), with a shared envelope carrying `event_id`, `event_type`, `event_time`, `business_date`, `schema_version`, and context ids.

```
sales.order.opened · sales.order_line.added / .updated / .voided / .fired
sales.table.transferred / .merged · sales.qr_session.started
sales.order.submitted_by_guest / .confirmed_by_staff / .rejected_by_staff
kitchen.ticket.bumped / .recalled
billing.bill.split / .merged / .settled / .voided
billing.discount.applied · billing.comp.applied · billing.payment.captured
billing.tip.adjusted · billing.refund.issued
cash.shift.opened / .closed · cash.drawer.opened / .paid_in / .paid_out
inventory.item.sold_out / .restored
inventory.stock.consumed / .adjusted / .counted
promotion.voucher.redeemed
delivery.shipment.created / .status_changed
config.version.published · device.activation.completed · fleet.update.rolled_out
```

**Thirteen further types complete the set.** Each exists because a rule stated elsewhere in
this document had no event able to carry it, and the asymmetry of the naming standard is
what makes declaring them early correct: adding an event type is additive and free, while
removing one is forbidden. So the cost of declaring one now is near zero, and the cost of
discovering a missing one after a thousand stores hold offline data is a protocol version
bump.

```
sales.order.closed · sales.order_line.held
sales.table.opened / .closed
billing.bill.opened · billing.receipt.reprinted · billing.fee.waived
cash.shift.counted
inventory.stock.received / .wasted
promotion.voucher.reserved
security.permission.overridden
config.version.activated
```

| Type | The rule that needs it |
|---|---|
| `security.permission.overridden` | §11.4 makes a manager-PIN override above a ceiling a fraud control. Nothing carried it, so the control had **no auditable record at all** |
| `cash.shift.counted` | §6 requires the close to be **blind** — the count entered before the expected amount is revealed. Folded into `shift.closed`, the blindness is unverifiable afterwards, which defeats the control |
| `inventory.stock.wasted` | §8: cancelling a fired line records waste and does *not* return stock. The ledger has waste entries; the catalogue had no waste event |
| `inventory.stock.received` | §8 names receipt as one of the five ledger entry kinds |
| `promotion.voucher.reserved` | §7 makes redemption an atomic check-and-mark, so reserve and redeem are distinct states. With only redemption, a settlement that then failed would burn the voucher |
| `billing.bill.opened` | Split, merged, settled and voided all presuppose a bill that nothing created |
| `billing.receipt.reprinted` | §11.4 counts every reprint and §11.3 puts each employee's reprint rate on the dashboard. A copy printed with no event could be counted by nothing |
| `billing.fee.waived` | §5 makes waiving a fee an act on one bill, with a reason and a permission ([ADR-0159](adr/0159-a-fee-is-configuration.md) decision 5). A fee left off a bill with no event would be money forgiven that nothing records. It carries `bill_id`, `fee_id` and `reason_code_id`, ids only |
| `sales.order.closed`, `sales.order_line.held` | §3 requires hold; the order lifecycle had no terminal event distinct from settlement |
| `sales.table.opened`, `sales.table.closed` | §2's table cycles through five states; only two transitions had events |
| `config.version.activated` | Published is not the same as running. The fleet view needs to know which store is actually on which version |

**A bill records its fees** ([ADR-0159](adr/0159-a-fee-is-configuration.md) decisions 3 and 4), in fields of the types above. `billing.bill.opened` carries `fee_rules`: the rules in force for the bill when it opened, frozen, each with every field the bill is computed from — `fee_id`, `code`, `display_name`, `kind`, `rate` or `amount`, `item_scope` and `menu_item_ids`, `base_discounted`, `base_tax_inclusive`, `tax`, `tax_class_id` and `waivable`. A split part's are its source's, with a fee per bill cut to the part's share (§5). `billing.bill.settled` carries `fee_lines` (`fee_id`, `code`, `display_name`, `amount`, `tax`), one per fee charged in rule order, and `tax_lines` (`tax_class_id`, `taxable_base`, `rate_basis_points`, `tax`), one per class, summing to `tax_total`. The `display_name` is the rule's own, frozen as a line's name is, and no event carries a translation: a receipt looks one up by `fee_id` in the rules the store runs when it prints (§12). Each field is left out when empty. A missing `fee_rules` or `fee_lines` means none; a missing `tax_lines` means *not recorded*, not *no tax*, so a reader takes the tax from `tax_total`. *The edge writes `fee_rules` at every bill open, empty while no `fees` node is published, and every settle records its `tax_lines`, and its `fee_lines` when it charged a fee. A copy of the receipt prints from them (§11.4).*

This catalogue is the single source for chain reporting, reconciliation, ERP posting, and webhooks. It is rendered to `docs/snapshots/events.txt` from the code, and CI refuses any removal from that file.

## 19. Scope

**Excluded on purpose**, each for a stated reason: reservations and waitlists (demand unproven) · buffet and time-based billing · CRM, loyalty, and marketing (separate domain; the public API lets tenants connect an existing system) · multi-currency within one bill · self-service kiosks and online storefronts (`POST /v1/orders` already lets anyone build them) · gift cards (needs an online balance ledger like vouchers; the payment-method slot is reserved) · payroll and full purchase orders (ERP territory) · **training mode** (§11.6) · payment processing — we integrate terminals and stay acquirer-neutral, which costs us transaction revenue and buys the operator freedom from lock-in.
